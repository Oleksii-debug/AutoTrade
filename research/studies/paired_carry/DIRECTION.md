# Directional exposure and the weekly target

The registered weekly trend rule on BTC, ETH and SOL perpetuals lost 16.30 and 106.63 USDT on two independently initialized 1,000 USDT accounts. The inverse-direction control lost 5.59 USDT in the first period and gained 88.32 in the second. The strongest recent control held a short of approximately 200 USDT per market, with no borrowing or resizing. Its two 26-week stress replays gained 179.60 and 106.23 USDT. The second period still sustained a 25.55% drawdown on hourly marks.

The follow-up held the short control fixed and tested three preceding 26-week blocks. The simulated returns were −125.51, +70.19 and +28.74 USDT per 1,000. Across all five independent blocks, 39 of 130 weeks reached 2%, 68 weeks finished positive, and the median week gained 0.226%. The worst week lost 12.56%. The older experiments used midnight UTC weekly marks, and the two recent ones used 01:00 UTC. Each accounted for observed funding and fees and adverse execution at every entry and exit. The short-only result depends on market direction. No live position was opened from these historical results.

Run `python trend_sides.py` and `python short_control.py` after the paired funding and daily spot datasets are present. Regenerate the receipts from the hashed public source packets. Numerical weekly results are on total account equity.

`python direction_watch.py` observes matched long and short controls from a preregistered ten-minute entry window on November 20 at 15:00 UTC. The same Windows task that checks BTC funding invokes this observer. Each virtual account starts at 1,000 USDT and uses identical sizes priced from opposite sides of the three real order books. The ledger preserves all funding and matched weekly outcomes. Each missing hourly observation is counted. The current values are readable in `direction-forward/Два рахунки.txt`. Run `python test_direction_watch.py` to check the fixed entry window, two-sided accounting, gap handling, weekly comparison and idempotent restart.
